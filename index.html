Recipes · real-world N5 NAS

Pick by workload — then by OS.

AI-first box (RAG-like)

Hot NVMe: btrfs-raid5 (bat) / raidz1 (Proxmox default)

Weight + embedding/vector IO streams off a hot NVMe pool. Prefer seqRD and capacity (Pareto on capacity∩sequential read). AI bat is provisional on btrfs-raid5 — directional tok/s only. Vector upsert/index rebuilds also care about seqWR; rnd4kW matters less than for VMs unless your store is write-amplified.

Thesis / gap: cold 5×3.5″ SATA is not in this recipe’s critical path for RAG hot weights. If embeddings spill to cold tier, that path is unmeasured — do not invent HDD numbers.

AI + VM server (+ cold SATA)

Hot: raid10 (ZFS) or btrfs-raid10 for VMs/DB · AI weights on capacity layout beside it

Split the story: VM disks / databases want rnd4kW + sync (mirrors win on ZFS stage1 cold); the AI weight tree still wants capacity∩seqRD. One box can host both — do not collapse them into a single “best RAID.”

Cold SATA (explicit gap): the N5’s unbenchmarked 5×3.5″ SATA bay is a thesis for cold datasets, backups, and model archives — no sealed fio cells. Hypotheses only: sequential archive reads may be spindle-bound; random 4k from HDD will not resemble NVMe rnd4kW; do not paste hot-tier GB/s onto the cold bay. By OS / NAS stack

Recommended layout follows the ecosystem — not only the slider.

Proxmox / ZFS-first

raidz1 recommended default

Stay in the ZFS/Proxmox toolchain. raidz1 is the sealed capacity∩seqRD default (14.11 TiB · 7.83 GB/s cold). btrfs-raid5 remains fastest measured if someone deliberately leaves that ecosystem and owns RAID5 rebuild + write-hole risk.

stage1-raidz1-full · STO-01 · 7.83 winner-selection · recommended default = raidz1

Fedora / Ubuntu (btrfs-accessible)

Hypothesis: btrfs-raid5 can win outright on measured speed

Outside Proxmox, newer distro kernels make btrfs operationally easy — and cold sealed numbers favor btrfs-raid5 on capacity∩seqRD (14.43 TiB · 9.03 GB/s). That is a speed argument, not a “Fedora fixed the write-hole” claim.

Write-hole honesty: upstream Status still rates RAID56 unstable (btrfs.readthedocs.io Status, kernel 7.2 table). Lab cells flag FSB-BTRFS-12 (write_hole_exposed: true for data parity. Metadata is mitigated with -m raid1c3 (FSB-BTRFS-04) — that does not close the data write-hole. Raid-stripe-tree RAID5 remains experimental / RFC — not a distro “it’s fixed” story. We documented the caveat; we did not crash-inject unclean shutdown. Prefer btrfs-raid10 if you need btrfs without parity write-hole exposure.

N5 Lab analysis · first pass

Joins from sealed metrics — not only the dual-answer slogan.

Claude’s winner stance is the right dual headline: btrfs-raid5 is fastest measured on the cold capacity∩seqRD Pareto; raidz1 is the recommended default when the operator’s world is Proxmox/ZFS. N5 Lab’s interactive pass keeps that stance and then asks what the matrix implies once you stop treating “recommended” as a single scalar.

Capacity-matched race is a write-path story

At ~14 TiB usable, btrfs-raid5 cold seqWR (~4.07 GB/s) far outruns raidz1 (~0.72 GB/s) while seqRD stays near line rate on both (9.03 vs 7.83). The capacity∩seqRD Pareto therefore understates how much the write stack differs. For RAG weight staging and embedding bulk load, that seqWR gap is operationally louder than the ~1.2 GB/s seqRD delta — still without promoting warm DRAM numbers as proof. stage5-btrfs-raid5 · STO-01_seq_write_1M_j4 · stage1-raidz1-full

VM co-tenancy flips the axis set

Once the box also runs VMs/DB, rnd4kW and sync dominate. ZFS raid10 cold rnd4kW 0.167 vs raidz1 0.077, sync 0.027 vs 0.007 — mirrors earn their keep. An “AI+VM” recipe that picks raidz1 for everything is optimizing the weight tree and neglecting the guest disk path. Split pools or accept raid10’s capacity tax on the hot tier. stage1-raid10-full vs stage1-raidz1-full · STO-02 / STO-04

Lane tax ≠ sync tax

Gen4 ×4 vs ×1 delivers ~3.27× seqRD but barely moves sync (~1.1×). Putting a SLOG on the ×4 (lab #57) is a sync-shaped bet; it will not “unlock” sequential read the way a wider data stripe would. Flush-bound paths need a different lever than PCIe width. x1_vs_x4 · STO-01 / STO-04

What we did not measure (and must not invent)

The 5×3.5″ SATA cold bay has zero sealed Protocol B cells in the public scrub. Any cold-tier GB/s, rebuild toll on spindles, or “archive is fine on HDD RAIDZ” claim is hypothesis pending seal. Separately: btrfs write-hole is labeled (FSB-BTRFS-12) but not crash-injected — integrity impact beyond speed/redundancy is an open measurement gap, not a resolved “mitigated by raid1c3 metadata” story for data.

OS recipes are risk-shaped, not chart-shaped

TrueNAS → ZFS path (raidz1 default). Proxmox → same. Fedora/Ubuntu → btrfs may win on sealed speed, but upstream RAID56 remains unstable and our lab did not close the write-hole with a fault arm. The interactive sliders help explore weights; they do not absolve write-hole or unmeasured cold SATA.
